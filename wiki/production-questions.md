### B2. Questions to ask production (pricing)

11. **Cost per unit by size** (40ml / 80ml / 100ml) — landed cost including oil, bottle, label, packaging?
12. What is the **minimum viable margin per unit** at our planned wholesale prices?
13. Can we hit a **3-tier wholesale ladder**: budget (~R30), mid (~R80–100), premium (~R150–200)?
14. What is the **agent markup headroom** at each tier (2×)? Does R60→R120 / R100→R200 / R180→R360 sell in townships?
15. What is the **production cost for a branded/Oud line** vs generic dupes? (is the premium tier worth it?)
16. **Volume discounts** — what wholesale price do we get at 100 / 500 / 1,000 units?
17. **Pricing for gift sets** — can they be priced to sell at R90–R150 retail (m-scents benchmark)?
18. What are the **payment terms** with production (prepay vs net-30) and how does that affect our working capital?

## C. CUSTOMER PROFILE — THE STORES' DUAL MODEL

### C1. The key observation

M-Scents (21 stores) and physical retailers sell to **TWO distinct buyers from the same shelf**:

| | **Individual consumer** (walk-in) | **Reseller / agent** (business buyer) |
|---|---|---|
| What they buy | 1–2 bottles, impulse, gift | 20–50 bottles, stock for resale |
| What they pay | Retail: R150–R420 (bottle) | Reseller: R80–R200 (bottle) |
| What drives them | Smell, brand trust, price vs mall | Margin %, sellability, sell-through speed |
| Purchase frequency | Occasional / gifting | Weekly–monthly restock |
| Decision | In-store sniff test | Trust in brand + past sales |
| Sensitivity | Mid (wants a deal, not cheapest) | High (unit economics matter) |

### C2. Questions to validate the profile

19. In a given store, what % of revenue is walk-in individuals vs resellers? (m-scents store managers would know)
20. Which price point do individual consumers in township malls actually pay most often — R150 or R300?
21. Do resellers re-buy the SAME bestsellers or rotate to new scents? (informs SKU breadth vs depth)
22. What's the **average transaction size** for each customer type?
23. How many of m-scents' 241 SKUs actually move fast? (likely a "long tail" — 20% of SKUs = 80% of sales. Which 20%?)
24. **What does a 2-MONTH BUYER CYCLE look like?** (buyer behaviour — we need to understand this before committing to stock):
    - Do consumers re-buy the SAME scent in month 2 (loyalty) or switch to new ones?
    - Do resellers reorder in month 2 from the same bestsellers, or after their initial stock sells out — and how long does initial stock last?
    - When do reorders spike? (payday / month-end / after social grants — timing matters for cash flow + stock)
    - What share of month-1 buyers are STILL buying in month 2 (retention) vs one-off?
    - Do buyers step UP tiers over the cycle (budget → signature → premium), or stay flat?
    - How does the 2-month cycle inform our monthly flyer cadence + when we restock/what we feature?

### C3. Critical assumptions we can make (flagged for validation)

1. **ASSUMPTION 1 — Dual demand exists at every physical point.** A store that serves individuals + resellers from one shelf proves both demand curves coexist. Therefore Zen's agent network needs BOTH a retail-facing catalogue (for consumers) and a margin-facing wholesale ladder (for resellers).
2. **ASSUMPTION 2 — Branded portfolio drives consumer trust; price drives reseller trust.** m-scents uses registered brands (Motala/P2D) to sell to individuals and price ladder to sell to resellers. We need both levers.
3. **ASSUMPTION 3 — Township/peri-urban consumers pay R150–R300 retail, not the R500+ mall brands.** m-scents' 150 Range at R150 reseller → ~R250–300 retail sits squarely in the township budget band. Our pricing must target R100–R300 retail.
4. **ASSUMPTION 4 — Sizes matter.** m-scents spans 15ml–200ml. A 15–30ml affordable tester/impulse SKU (R40–80 retail) and a 100ml hero SKU are both needed. Single-size (our current plan) is a gap.
5. **ASSUMPTION 5 — Resellers restock fast-moving scents, not broad ranges.** SKU breadth for discovery, SKU depth for the proven ~20 bestsellers. Launch lean, restock deep.
6. **ASSUMPTION 6 — Gift sets are a real, high-margin category.** m-scents sells R90 gift sets; FFC doesn't. Gifting is a separate buying trigger we currently ignore.

## D. GEOGRAPHY CONSIDERATION

### D1. What competitor store locations tell us about demand geography

- **M-Scents = KZN township/peri-urban malls only** (Edendale, Tugela Ferry, Umzimkhulu, Jozini, Kokstad, Durban CBD). This proves township/peri-urban demand exists and is profitable enough to support 21 physical stores.
- **FFC = national cities** (warehouse-per-major-city) — services agents nationally, thin in townships.
- **Mthatha (EC) signal** — M-Scents' reported first Eastern Cape store = EC township demand is under-served.
- **Gauteng townships = nobody** (FFC = city warehouses, M-Scents = not in GP).

### D2. Geographic questions

24. Does the customer profile differ by geography? (e.g., Durban CBD consumer vs Soweto consumer vs Mthatha consumer — price sensitivity, brand preference, size preference)
25. Which geography has the **highest density of active resellers** per capita? (townships > suburbs, based on m-scents' township mall strategy)
26. Does **retail price acceptance** vary by province/area? (should Zen price differently in KZN vs GP vs EC?)
27. Should Zen's **agent-recruitment first wave** mirror m-scents' proven township-mall geographies, but in Gauteng + EC where they're absent?

### D3. Critical geographic assumption

**ASSUMPTION 7 — The township/peri-urban market is the core engine.** m-scents proves it supports 21 physical stores in one province. A WhatsApp/agent model needs NO stores — so the same demand can be captured with far lower capital, in the geographies competitors haven't touched (Gauteng townships, Eastern Cape).
